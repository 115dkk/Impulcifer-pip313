                    Room Correction
                    Compensate the room's frequency response.
                  
        
                  
                
        
                
        
                  
                  
        
                    
                    
                  
        
                  
        
                    
        
                      
                      
                      
                    
        
                    
        
                  
        
                  
        
                    Correction mode:Corrects the frequency response at each ear position; the timing of the bass stays as recorded.
                    
        
                      
                      
                      
                    
        
                  
        
                  
                  
        
                    PEQ and an FIR with 8 ms latency. The FIR corrects part of the room's bass timing within that latency.
                    
                  
        
                  
        
                    
                    
                  
        
                  
        
                    
                    
                  
        
                  
        
                    
                    
                  
        
                  
        
                    Auto multiplies the Schroeder frequency of the chosen basis by the reach, with a minimum of 100 Hz and a maximum of 500 Hz times the reach. Without a room volume or a Schroeder frequency it is 500 Hz.
                    
                  
        
                  
        
                    Strict uses the room decay at 500 Hz and 1 kHz. Lenient also measures the decay in third-octave bands from 100 to 500 Hz and takes the highest band whose own Schroeder estimate reaches it; it is never below the strict value. Treated rooms usually ring longer in the bass, so lenient goes higher there.
                    
                  
        
                  
        
                    Multiplies the Schroeder frequency to correct further into the transition region above it. ×2 allows up to 1 kHz and ×4 up to 2 kHz.
                    
                  
        
                  
        The basis and the reach need the room volume or a Schroeder frequency below. Without them the limit stays at 500 Hz.
        
                  
        
                    Corrects up to four more ranges above the main band, written as low-high pairs in Hz and separated by commas, for example 1000-3000. Each range fades in over half an octave below its low edge and out over half an octave above its high edge. Leave empty for none.
                    
                  
        
                  
        
                    An 8th-order high-pass at this frequency on every speaker, 10 to 50 Hz, applied after the correction is designed. All speakers pass the same filter, so their phase relation above the cut stays the same while the deepest bass is not driven. Leave empty for none.
                    
                  
        
                  
        
                    
                    
                    A dip that falls below the response's one-octave envelope is filled by 2 dB plus half of the rest, 6 dB at most, instead of up to the boost limit. Narrow cancellations then take less boost; broad losses are still corrected in full. The limited dips are listed in the README.
                  
        
                  
        
                    
                    
                    Finds where the speaker's own response rolls off (10 dB below its 200 to 1000 Hz level) and allows no boost below that frequency, with the boost limit ramping up over the octave above it. The PEQ bands and the FIR are checked afterwards so no boost leaks below the rolloff. Without a rolloff above 20 Hz nothing changes.
                  
        
                  
        
                    
                    
                    Finds room modes below 160 Hz that ring well past the room's 200 to 500 Hz decay and adds a cut at each, at most four, so the mode dies away sooner instead of only getting less boost. Works with every filter. The cuts are listed in the README.
                  
        
                  
        
                    
                    
                    Below about 200 Hz the FIR moves each speaker's bass so it arrives with its treble; below the speaker's own rolloff the bass keeps that rolloff's delay. The bass is pulled forward by at most one and a half periods, which keeps the pre-ringing in check. The 8 ms filter usually cannot pull the full amount.
                  
        
                  
        
                    
                    
                    Above the correction band the FIR leaves a fixed phase turn that differs from speaker to speaker. This undoes it over the three octaves above the band, only by adding delay there, so every speaker's treble keeps the same phase.
                  
        
                  
        PEQ only has no FIR, so the bass arrival and the treble phase stay as recorded.
        
                  
                  
        
                    Makes the response flat up to the Schroeder frequency, including dips a real room EQ cannot fill.
                    
                  
        
                  
        
                    
                    
                  
        
                  
        
                    Above this frequency only a broad tone correction is applied; the detailed correction fades out over the next octave.
                    
                  
        
                  
        
                    
                    
                    Matches the speakers' levels at the listening position within ±6 dB, as a room-correction processor does. Turn it off to keep the levels as recorded.
                  
        
                  
        
                    
                    
                    Adds to each speaker low-frequency copies played by the other speakers, designed to cancel the bass that lingers at the ears after the first 60 ms (adjustable below). Works only below the upper limit, and a check keeps every band from getting worse.
                  
        
                  
        
                    
                    
                  
        
                  
        
                    
                    
                  
        
                  
        
                    How long after the direct sound the MIMO copies may start to act. A shorter time lets them also shorten the early bass decay, but drives them harder, so the check turns them down more often. 60 ms is the earlier behaviour.
                    
                  
        
                  
        
                    
                    
                    The check that turns the MIMO copies down looks at both ears together, so a copy that helps one ear more than it hurts the other gets through. With this on, the copies are turned down whenever either ear gets worse. The README names the ear that set the limit.
                  
        
                  
        
                    Up to the Schroeder frequency, the room's bass timing is corrected. Higher limits also correct the speaker's own timing, for example around its crossover, where the room measurements at both ear positions agree. Off corrects the frequency response only and adds no delay.
                    
        
                      
                        
                      
                      Full band
                    
        
                  
        
                  
        
                    
                    
                    Checked: only the room's bass timing up to the Schroeder frequency is corrected. Unchecked: timing is corrected over the full band, where the room measurements at both ear positions agree.

        Formats to save even with speakers missing:

        Only hrir.wav and hesuvi.wav are saved.
        
                    
        
                  
        
                  
                  
        
                    
                    
        
                      
                      
        For hesuvi.wav, hrir.wav and the channel-order files. The nearest measured speaker on the same side and at the same height is copied, and a missing center speaker is made from the pair beside it. Height channels are never filled from ear-level speakers.
        
                    
        
                  
        
                  
        
                    
                    
        
                      
                      
        The LFE tracks of the channel-order files stay silent. hesuvi.wav and hrir.wav have no LFE track.
        
                      
        The LFE tracks use the center speaker, or the two front speakers when the center was not measured.
        
                      
        The LFE tracks are made from the bass of every measured speaker below 120 Hz. Averaging the directions evens out the peaks and dips of any one position, as several subwoofers do in a room.

              04
              Virtual Bass
            
        
            
        
              
        
                
        
                  
                  
        
                    Enable virtual bass
                    Extend low-end response below the crossover.
                  
        
                  
                
        
                
        
                  
        
                    Finds where the speakers lose bass and puts the crossover one octave above it.
                    
                  
        
                  
        
                    Room correction leaves everything below the crossover to virtual bass, except with the Legacy (2.x) range.
